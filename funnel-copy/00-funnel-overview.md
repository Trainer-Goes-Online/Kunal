## SELF-CHALLENGE (arguing the opposite of my main verdict)

**My verdict:** withholding the program price across all four pages and only naming the assessment fee is correct for this high-ticket application funnel.

**The counter I considered:** A skeptical ₹1Cr+ businessman is time-poor and price-anchored. Hiding the price entirely could read as coy or as a bait-and-switch, and paying even a small fee to *find out the price* may feel like a runaround — costing conversions from exactly the highest-value men who value directness.

**Does it hold?** Partly. It's a real risk, so I mitigated rather than dismissed it: the landing page names the four costs and explicitly says money is "a conversation Kunal has with you directly" (naming the withholding openly, not hiding it), the checkout justifies *why* the assessment is paid (respects both clocks), and the reassurance is that it's a working hour, not a pitch. The offer doc is explicit that application-not-checkout is the model and that "no discounts / no instalment menus" signal a real price — so the strategy is source-mandated, not my invention. **Verdict stands, with one dependency:** it holds ONLY if the assessment-fee terms (credited/refundable) are confirmed and shown. If the fee is non-refundable AND not credited, the runaround risk becomes real and the checkout copy must be softened. Flagged below.

**Strongest counter left on the table:** for the very top of the market, consider a single ROI/"pays for itself" line on the landing page to pre-empt the price question without naming a number — not added, because the offer doc's tone is deliberately understated. Optional A/B test, not a correction.
